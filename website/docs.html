Install on Android

Gym Plus (package com.gympulse.app) installs as a normal Android app. Release APKs are not published on this site yet; until then, build it from source (see Build it yourself) and install the APK:

  1. Copy the APK to your phone and open it.
  2. Your phone will ask whether the app you opened it with may install apps — allow it. This is standard for any app installed outside the Play Store (called sideloading), and you can revoke the permission right after.
  3. Confirm, done. No account, no setup.
The openGym.apk offered on the upstream openGym site is the original openGym app by Duarte Santos, not Gym Plus. Both can be installed side by side — they have different package ids.

Everything is stored on the phone itself. The only things fetched from the internet are the exercise images and animations (from a CDN, on demand) — plus Google Drive and your AI provider, only if you turn those on.

Use on iPhone

Gym Plus has a native iOS project (bundle id com.gympulse.app). Without an App Store release, the options are:

  • Build it onto your own iPhone: with a Mac and Xcode, the native app runs on your device with a free Apple ID (Apple expires the signature after 7 days; re-run from Xcode to renew). See docs/MOBILE.md.
  • Self-host + home screen: open your own instance in Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen. You get a full-screen app with its own icon, passkey sign-in and server sync.

Google Drive sync (optional)

In the mobile app, Profile → Sign in with Google turns on sync. Your log is stored as one file, gymplus-data.json, in a visible Gym Plus folder in your own Google Drive. Gym Plus only asks for access to files it created itself (the drive.file scope) — it cannot see the rest of your Drive.

  • Signing in is optional; without it the app works fully offline, exactly as before.
  • Changes sync automatically, plus on launch and when you return to the app. Offline? It retries later.
  • On first sign-in with data on both sides you choose: merge, keep the cloud copy, or keep this phone's copy.

Building the app yourself? Google sign-in needs your own OAuth client ids — see docs/GOOGLE_SETUP.md.

AI coach (optional, bring your own key)

The coach reviews your training and proposes plan changes you accept or reject. In the mobile app it talks directly to the AI provider you choose — OpenAI, Anthropic (Claude), Google Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter — with your own API key.

  • The key is stored only on the phone (Keychain / Android Keystore). It is never synced, never sent anywhere except to that provider, and never logged.
  • Pick the provider and model under Profile → AI coach; you can add your own standing instructions for the coach.
  • Provider usage is billed by the provider to your key.

Self-host with Docker

The self-hosted flavor adds passkey profiles, sync across devices, and (optionally) an admin dashboard. You need Docker with Compose. From a checkout of the Gym Plus repository:

cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build

Open http://localhost:8080, tap Create profile, and you're in. The first launch downloads the exercise media (~140 MB) once.

Want it reachable from your phone with passkeys? You'll need an HTTPS domain — a two-line change in .env. The full guide, including reverse-proxy examples and the invite-only/admin options, is in docs/SELF_HOSTING.md (inherited from upstream openGym).

Import your training history

Coming from another tracker? Settings → Data → Import from another app reads:

  • FitNotes (both the Android and the FitNotes 2 iOS export)
  • Strong and Hevy (CSV exports — session lengths carry over, so the activity heatmap fills in properly)
  • Apple Health (body-weight history from the XML export)
  • …and anything else with date, exercise-name and weight/reps columns

Exercise names are matched against the 1,324-exercise library; anything genuinely ambiguous becomes one of your own custom exercises instead of being guessed at. A summary shows exactly what will happen before anything is written, and importing is idempotent — running it twice never duplicates a workout.

Backups

Settings → Data → Export backup (JSON) writes your entire log — plan, workouts, weights, settings — into one file. On the mobile app it goes out through the share sheet (Files, AirDrop, mail…); in the browser it downloads. Import backup restores it anywhere: another phone, your self-hosted instance, or a fresh install. Backups are compatible with openGym.

Build it yourself

  • Web / self-host: docker compose up -d --build builds the frontend inside the container — no local Node needed.
  • Android APK / iOS app: the Capacitor projects live in frontend/android and frontend/ios — build, signing and distribution notes are in docs/MOBILE.md; Google sign-in setup in docs/GOOGLE_SETUP.md.

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes — free as in cost and as in freedom (AGPL-3.0). No pro tier, no locked features, no ads. The AI coach uses your own provider key, so any AI costs are between you and that provider.

What data does Gym Plus collect?

None. There is no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting. Without sign-in the mobile app's only network traffic is fetching exercise media from a CDN. With sign-in, your log goes to your Google Drive; with the coach on, the training summary it needs goes to the AI provider you picked.

How is Gym Plus related to openGym?

Gym Plus is based on openGym by Duarte Santos (AGPL-3.0). It keeps openGym's tracker and adds a mobile-first build with optional Google Drive sync and a bring-your-own-key AI coach.

Where does the exercise database come from?

From hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset (CC-licensed): 1,324 exercises with animations and instructions in 10 languages.
